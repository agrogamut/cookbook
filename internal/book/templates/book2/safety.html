Before you cook

Kitchen safety & hygiene

Food-safety practice behind every recipe in this book, drawn from the provider's own safety standard operating procedures. Most of the rules below trace to two named public-health frameworks: the World Health Organization's "Five Keys to Food Safety" (keep clean, separate raw and cooked, cook thoroughly, keep food at safe temperatures, use safe water and raw materials), and CDC food-safety guidance for preparing food for young children. Where a rule's citation has not yet resolved to a full reference (a known gap -- GAP-028), it still prints without one rather than with a guessed source.

The general practice behind these rules

Whatever you are cooking, the same handful of habits do most of the work: wash your hands and surfaces before you start; keep raw meat, fish and eggs away from food that is ready to eat; cook food through rather than leaving any part underdone; do not leave prepared food sitting at room temperature for long; and use water you would drink yourself. The specific rules below are how those habits apply to this book's recipes.
