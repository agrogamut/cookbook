## 1. Orientation: what the workbooks actually are

The first thing established, because the naming misleads.

**"Book 1" and "Book 2" mean two different things in this project** and it is worth being
explicit about which is meant:

| Meaning | What it refers to |
|---|---|
| **Input workbooks** | `MadamGY_Book1_Content_Master_V1_1_DailyLife.xlsx` and `MadamGY_Book2_Content_Master_V1.xlsx` - two of the thirteen provider spreadsheets |
| **Output books** | The two personalized PDFs the Book Engine produces for each child - the actual product |

The input workbooks are named after the output books they feed, but they are not the same
thing and they are not parallel in content:

- `Book1_Content_Master` holds 32 daily-life guidance blocks, 44 vaccine rows and 33
  milestone rows. Parenting content. Almost nothing to do with recipes.
- `Book2_Content_Master` holds, among other sheets, `Recipe Selection Logic` - **the
  authoritative 14-step engine specification**. This is the sheet the whole engine
  implements.

### The flow is not input → Book 1 → Book 2

It is:

```
13 provider workbooks  ->  importer  ->  Postgres
                                            |
child profile (age, diet, allergy, clinical, region, budget)
                                            |
                                      14-step engine
                                            |
                            ranked recipes + step accounting
                                            |
                          [Phase 3, not built] Book 1 + Book 2 PDFs
```

### What is actually imported

`internal/importer/spec.go` binds **21 tables from 10 workbooks**. Three workbooks
contribute nothing:

`Book1_Content_Master` contributing zero is the significant one: it is the **entire general

## 2. The provider's Phase 3 delivery

Nine files, 17 August, defining a "Knowledge Book Engine". Fully summarised in
`docs/phase-3-book-engine.md`. They now live in `data/book-engine-spec/`; the provider's
13 workbooks moved to `data/provider/` at the same time. The headline points:

- Every input produces **exactly two books**. Fixed output contract, and it is the product.
- Book 1 is guidance and monitoring (~70-130+ pages), no recipe methods. Book 2 is recipes
  (~160-350+ pages).
- Deterministic safety and clinical logic is separated from generative assistance. The
  ordering rule (SRS Appendix B) must not be reversed: safety → clinical rules → nutrition
  targets → recipe eligibility → ranking → content assembly → language assistance → human
  review → release.
- New service with its own API (`/v1/book-engine/*`), connected to the existing
  consultation software over REST rather than merged with it.
- Asynchronous, job-based generation driven by an 11-state machine.
- Five human review gates plus a release owner. Immutable release records with file hashes.
- A second ranking rubric (weights 30/20/15/10/8/7/5/5, duplicate penalty to -20) that
  does not obviously reconcile with the NT00-NT12 rubric already implemented.

**The Phase 2 engine is the "Recipe Engine" box in their architecture.** Reusable, not
superseded. Everything upstream and downstream of it is unbuilt.

## 3. The personalization question

**Asked:** the general (non-specialized) content should still feel personal, and every
input on the website should produce these two books.

**Answered** in `docs/phase-3-book-engine.md` §3. Summary:

Four legitimate levers - conditional section firing, slot filling into provider-authored
templates, selection among provider-authored variants, ordering by the child's goals. None
generate prose.

But the lever that actually carries the feeling is **the child's own measured data on the
page**. The provider's own Book 1 prototype proves it: page 3 reads `16.2 kg`, `103 cm`,
`Irregular breakfast`, `Review after 6 weeks` - intensely personal, zero generated
sentences. Personalization density is a data-on-page metric, not a prose-volume metric.

**Ruling made:** no generated guidance prose reaches a parent through a path with no human
review gate. Narrower than the provider's own SRS, deliberately - their permission for
"editorial variation and parent-friendly phrasing" (SRS 18.1) depends on five review gates
this project does not have. Gate placement, not technology. Revisit when the review portal
exists. Recorded in `docs/decisions.md`.

**The blocker nobody has solved:** the general content pool is too thin for any of this.
Book 1's content master is unimported; Book 2's method text is 6 unique strings across
1000 recipes. Both are authoring problems, not engineering ones. **Nobody has said how
many block variants get written, or by whom.** That single unassigned number decides
whether the books feel personal.

## 4. The input inventory

**Asked:** a complete list of everything that can be given to the engine.

**Answered** in `docs/engine-inputs.md`, with every vocabulary queried live from the

Also published as a browsable page:

That page is a convenience view. **`docs/engine-inputs.md` is the durable copy** - the
artifact is not version-controlled and should not be treated as the source of truth.

Headline findings from compiling it:

- The API accepts 13 fields; the UI form sends 7. `region_culture` and `cuisine_code` are
  the costly omissions - they are the whole of engine step 7.
- Five populated columns nothing asks for: `season`, `growth_target`,
  `post_vaccine_context`, `texture`, `clinical_tag`.
- **Four allergens filter nothing.** Tree nuts, crustacean/mollusc, mustard and sulphites
  have no corpus tag. Accepted, applied to nothing. This fails in the unsafe direction
  because the interface implies a protection that does not exist. Highest-priority item in
  `docs/not-built.md`.
- `meal_category_target` and `recipe_master.meal_type` disagree: three target categories
  have zero recipes, three meal types have no target. Book 2's chapters are built from the
  former, so three would generate empty.

## 5. The diet bug

**Asked, as a question about the artifact:** why is `Eggetarian` a separate diet type with
one recipe - isn't an eggetarian person also able to eat vegetarian food?

Yes. And the engine did not know that.

`recipe_master.diet_type` states what a dish **requires of whoever eats it**, not which
kind of eater it belongs to. Diet is nested:
`vegan ⊂ vegetarian ⊂ eggetarian ⊂ non-vegetarian`. The filter matched for equality.

| Declared | Was seeing | Should see |
|---|---|---|
| Vegetarian | 828 | 828 |
| Eggetarian | **1** | 829 |
| Non-vegetarian | **111** | 940 |

A non-vegetarian child was getting 12% of the corpus.

**Why every review missed it:** the failure was conservative. Equality never served a
family food their practice forbids - it only withheld food they could have eaten. The
persona suite asserts `minResults >= 1` because it was written to catch *filter collapse*,
the original blocker. A short list is not a collapse.

Fixed in `internal/engine/diet.go` via a hand-written `dietPermits` map. Unknown diet type
now returns `ErrInvalidProfile` → 400 rather than silently matching nothing. The Phase-1
persona query carried the same equality bug and was updated to match so the paths cannot
drift. Tests pin the nesting as a subset chain.

**Deferred deliberately:** a diet *ranker*. A family declaring non-vegetarian probably
wants non-vegetarian dishes ranked up, not merely permitted, or page one of their recipe
book is dal. The SRS's "preference match, weight 10" is the right home. Filtering
correctness and ranking preference are separable; the filter was the part that was wrong.

**Also learned from this:** the corpus holds exactly **one** recipe using any egg
ingredient - `MG-R-00692`, tagged 6-9 years. An eggetarian child under six gets zero egg
dishes and always will until the provider writes some.

## 7. What a later session should know

- **The database is disposable.** `docker start recipie-pg` brings back the last import;
  `scripts/dev_db.fish up` builds it fresh. Connection string is
  `postgres://recipie:recipie@localhost:55432/recipie?sslmode=disable`.
- **The integrity suite needs a real database.** Without `TEST_DATABASE_URL` it silently
  skips, so a green `go test ./...` on a machine with no docker proves less than it looks.
- **Counts in `docs/engine-inputs.md` were measured, not estimated.** Regeneration queries
  are at the bottom of that file. Re-run them after any re-import rather than trusting the
  numbers indefinitely.
- **The `gap_register` table is live and re-measured on every import.** 20 rows after
  migration 0012, 7 marked blocker. It counts missing *values*; `docs/not-built.md` records
  missing *capability and decisions*. Both are needed.
- **Nothing in the database may ship.** All 940 recipes are `Review_Status = Draft`, all
  406 ingredients `Needs Validation`. The SRS requires a `Release_Eligible` flag that does
  not exist in the schema, and if it did, nothing would qualify.

### The seven blocker-severity gaps, verbatim from `gap_register`

| Gap | Rows | What |
|---|---|---|
| `GAP-001` | 940 | 6 unique preparation texts across all recipes |
| `GAP-002` | 940 | 1 unique safety rule text; same for storage and adaptation |
| `GAP-003` | 273 | Zero iron-support recipes for 6-11 months |
| `GAP-004` | 4462 | Everything is Draft / Needs Validation |
| `GAP-017` | 4 | Allergen groups with no matching tag anywhere in the corpus |
| `GAP-019` | NULL (seed) | Congenital/complex conditions with no clinical_rule_master row |
| `GAP-020` | 0 | Specialist-tier clinical rules the engine's rule query drops before escalation can see them |

## 8. Open, in priority order

**The four unmapped allergens are settled, not open, as a decision - the picker is not
built yet.** The decision is to keep the four selectable rather than remove them: the
reference endpoint already returns all eleven allergen groups, these four included, each
carrying a `screens` flag, and `EngineResult.unscreened_allergens` reports them on every
engine result so a client can show a persistent "not screened - no corpus coverage" state
next to the field and on the results. No client renders that state yet - the console
picker is Task 7's work, not landed. `GAP-017` tracks the hole until the provider tags
the corpus - the remaining work there is theirs, not a decision anyone here still owes.
Left out of the numbered list below because the decision, at least, is no longer open.

1. **Who writes the content variants, and how many.** Blocks all Book 1 personalization.
2. **Which ranking rubric governs** - NT00-NT12 or the SRS component weights. Two rubrics

3. **The meal category name mismatch.** Three Book 2 chapters would generate empty.
4. **Licence position on the external recipe corpus.** Unstated upstream, currently
   supplying method text for 166 recipes.
5. **Clinical sign-off.** Nothing ships without it.
